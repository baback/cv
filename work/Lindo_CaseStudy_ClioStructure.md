## What is an agentic website platform?

An agentic website platform builds and edits entire websites through AI agents instead of drag-and-drop. A user describes what they want in plain language, and specialized agents generate the pages, structure, copy, and styling — then keep editing the live site on command. For the people who build websites for a living — agencies and freelancers — this collapses days of production work into minutes. As the web-design industry moves from hand-built to AI-built, the platforms that win are the ones professionals can actually trust with client work.

---

## Project Inception

Lindo launched as an all-in-one AI marketing tool — assets, social posts, scheduling, and a landing page builder. But the usage data pointed at one thing: people came to build a website from a prompt, and the power users doing it at volume were agencies and freelancers building for *paying clients*.

We saw a clear opportunity: stop being one more website generator in a crowded market, and become the platform an agency runs its business on. By rebuilding Lindo around agents, we aimed to:

**Concentrate the product:** move from a scattered all-in-one to the fastest way to build and manage client websites with AI.

**Solve the real pain point:** agencies don't fear "bad AI" — they fear handing client work to a system they can't see, control, or undo. Make agent automation something they trust.

**Set a strategic foundation:** establish a defensible position — a white-label agency platform with its own tier and monetization — that templated competitors couldn't easily copy.

---

## The Problem Statement

In a flooded market of AI builders, "another tool that makes websites" is a race to the bottom on price. Lindo had no defensible position, and the people getting the most value — agencies — weren't who the product was designed for.

The deeper problem surfaced when we moved to agents. Agencies aren't building their own sites; they're building for clients whose budget and reputation are on the line. So when an AI agent generates or rewrites a whole website on its own, the agency froze on one question: *Can I trust what this agent produced — and stay in control of what it changes — enough to put my name on it and ship it to my client?* An agency cannot deliver work it doesn't trust.

---

## The Solution: Lindo Agentic

Lindo became an agentic platform where eight specialized agents build and edit a complete, multi-page website from a single prompt — wrapped in a control layer that keeps the agency in charge the whole time. Instead of a black-box "ta-da," the user watches the build happen, sees exactly what changed, controls what the agent is allowed to touch, and can undo anything.

The platform was conceived with a clear vision: give agencies a system to generate, deliver, bill, and grow client sites under their own brand — automation they trust, not automation they fear.

## Explorations and Usability Testing

I moved fast to validate the riskiest assumption — that agencies would hand work to an agent — by testing real control patterns rather than polishing screens.

**What I tried and killed:**

- **"Just trust it" — fully autonomous, no visibility** → killed; hiding the work amplified the fear instead of earning trust.  
- **Ask everything up front in a long form before building** → killed; it broke the one-prompt magic.  
- **Build first, then make review and adjustment effortless** → kept, and shaped the final flow.

## Build Experience Usability Study

The goal was to make handing a build to the agent feel safe, not like a gamble. We asked agencies to walk through generating and then editing a site, and watched where confidence broke.

**Key findings were encouraging:** agencies found the visible, page-by-page build far less stressful than a blind spinner — seeing the agent work was the difference between "waiting anxiously" and "watching it happen." The reveal that invited editing reframed the output as a starting point they owned, not a final answer to accept or reject. The most valuable insight: control beats autonomy — the moment they knew they could see what changed and undo it, willingness to use the agent on real client work jumped.

---

## Edit & Control Usability Study

Once a site exists, editing it is the anxiety-inducing phase — this is a client's live site. We studied what agencies needed to feel safe letting an agent make changes.

**The study surfaced clear preferences:** agencies wanted to *scope* what the agent could touch before it ran, a plain-language summary of *what changed* after, and a reliable way back. Reversibility wasn't a nice-to-have — it was the precondition for using the agent at all.

## Monetization and model

I A/B-tested how we package and price. Testing hard vs. soft paywall and free-trial vs. 30-day money-back, the **soft paywall \+ money-back guarantee won** ‹confirm lift› — it converted better *and* built the trust agencies need before committing client budget. The agency tier (Elite) bundled the white-label stack, CRM, and billing into a plan priced for a business, not a hobbyist.

This approach was chosen based on:

**User preference:** agencies wanted to try the platform on real client work before committing — a guarantee lowered that risk.

**Market alignment:** a defensible agency tier separated us from commodity per-site builders.

**Scalability:** one agent engine producing coherent sites at volume let the model serve solo freelancers and high-volume studios alike.

---

## Measuring success

I evaluated Lindo through adoption, behavior, and the platform's real impact on how agencies work.

**Where it landed:** 30,000+ users (mostly agencies and freelancers) building 1,000,000+ websites and landing pages; a commodity AI builder repositioned into a monetized agency platform; and a ground-up rebuild into an agentic product running on eight agents. Most telling was the behavior change — agencies went from "I'm scared to let it touch my client's site" to using agent-driven building and editing as their default way to work.

**What I learned:** in agentic products, trust *is* the feature — autonomy without control just scares people. The best thing I designed wasn't a screen; it was the agency's confidence to hand over the work. And designing for someone delivering to *their* client raised the bar on every decision.
